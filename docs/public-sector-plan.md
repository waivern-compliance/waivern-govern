# Plan: public services under the Data Protection Act 2018, as amended

Status: decisions settled (30 October 2026), all as recommended. Phases 0–5 built.

## What is asked for

Govern handles the UK GDPR well for any organisation, public bodies included, and is
current with the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (`docs/duaa-plan.md`). What it does not
know is the Data Protection Act 2018's own regime for public services: the Schedule 1
conditions and appropriate policy documents that health and social care rely on, the
Schedule 2 and 3 exemptions they use when answering requests, the rules that bind a
public authority as such, and — for police, prosecutors, courts, prisons, probation and
youth offending — the whole of Part 3, law enforcement processing.

This plan covers those, for UK public services: healthcare, social work and social
care, education, and criminal justice. It is written against the Act as it stands now,
not as enacted.

### Part 3: law enforcement processing

Applies to a **competent authority** (s.30, Sch 7): police forces, the DPP, courts and
tribunals, probation, the Youth Justice Board, the Parole Board, youth offending teams,
private prison and escort contractors, government departments (covering prisons), and
now the Border Security Commander (2026) and the Information Commission itself — and to
anyone else so far as it has statutory functions for law enforcement purposes. The
purposes (s.31) are unchanged: preventing, investigating, detecting or prosecuting
offences and executing penalties, including safeguarding against threats to public
security.

## Where Govern stands

- **No public-authority setting.** Entities carry only `public_comms_provider` (PECR).
  A council or NHS trust can tick legitimate interests or recognised legitimate
  interests on the ROPA with no warning.
- **No data protection officer.** The approval workflows have a stage named "Data
  protection officer", filled by the `privacy_admin` role; nothing records who the DPO
  is, how to contact them or to whom they report, and the UK DPIA does not ask for the
  DPO's advice (Art 35(2)).
- **No Schedule 1.** The DPIA asks "Which Article 9 condition applies?" as free text;
  the ROPA records nothing about special category or criminal offence data. Article 10
  data is folded into special category data everywhere. No appropriate policy document
  exists anywhere.
- **Exemptions as free text.** A request's "exemption" decision is "DPA 2018 Schedule 2"
  plus a paragraph typed in. Nothing for Schedule 3, the serious harm test, the health
  professional's opinion, or the immigration exemption's recording duties. The library
  holds `uk.dpa2018.sch2` but nothing cites it.
- **No Part 3.** No competent authority, law enforcement purpose, Schedule 8 condition,
  categories of data subject, logging, Part 3 breach, rights restriction, automated
  decision or transfer. Govern's breach, request, automated-decision and transfer
  modules would all apply the UK GDPR's rules to a police force's investigations.
- **No data sharing agreements.** The agreements register holds Article 28 processor
  agreements only; sharing between controllers — the everyday tool of safeguarding
  partnerships and multi-agency working — has nowhere to live.
- **Regulator named "ICO"** on about 45 lines of the source; only the complaints module says
  "Information Commission".
- **Already right for public bodies:** the Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard,
  equality impact assessment and the public sector equality duty, public-sector risk
  scales, and every DUAA change to the UK GDPR.

## Decisions (settled 30 October 2026, all as recommended)

1. **Where the regime is recorded.** A police force processes its staff's data under the
   UK GDPR and its investigations under Part 3; a council's youth offending team is a
   competent authority while the rest of the council is not.
   *Recommendation:* a `regime` on each processing activity (`uk_gdpr` | `dpa_part3`),
   defaulting from the entity, with a `competent_authority` flag on the entity that only
   makes Part 3 available. Requests, breaches and assessments take their regime from the
   activities they concern, and say which they are applying.
2. **Public authority.** *Recommendation:* a `public_authority` flag on the entity (s.7
   is about the body). Where set: legitimate interests or recognised legitimate
   interests on an activity is a **red** gap (the law does not allow it as recorded, as
   for the Article 22B gaps), and a DPO is required.
3. **The data protection officer.** *Recommendation:* recorded on the organisation, with
   an optional override per entity: name, contact, the user it is (if a member), whom
   they report to, and whether the contact details are published. The workflows' "Data
   protection officer" stage goes to that person where they are a member. The UK DPIA
   gains "What did the DPO advise?" (Art 35(2)).
4. **The appropriate policy document.** Govern does not write it; it needs to know it
   exists and what it covers, because para 41 and s.61 turn on that.
   *Recommendation:* a small register: title, version, the Schedule 1 / Schedule 8
   conditions it covers, owner, last reviewed, and the document itself attached. Gaps:
   an activity relying on a condition no current document covers; a document not reviewed
   in twelve months; a document withdrawn less than six months after an activity stopped
   relying on it.
5. **How Schedule 1 is asked.** *Recommendation:* on the ROPA, a structured Article 9(2)
   point and, where that point needs one, the Schedule 1 paragraph; Article 10 data asked
   separately, with its Schedule 1 condition. The DPIA's free-text `special_condition`
   becomes the same structured choice, edited in place as the DUAA phases did.
6. **Logging (s.62).** Govern is not where logs are kept and should not ingest them.
   *Recommendation:* on each Part 3 activity, a statement per system of what is logged
   (the six operations), who reviews the logs and when they were last checked, with a
   gap where an automated system has no statement.
7. **The health professional's opinion (Sch 3).** *Recommendation:* a decision on the
   request recording the opinion: the professional's role, whether they are the
   appropriate one under para 2, the date, and the outcome. Withholding health data
   without one, or on one older than six months, is refused unless the reason for not
   re-consulting is given.
8. **Data sharing agreements.** *Recommendation:* a register of their own, separate from
   processor agreements: the parties (from the third-party register), purpose, each
   party's lawful basis and Schedule 1 condition, the legal gateway (an enactment, such
   as the Digital Economy Act 2017 s.35, or the parties' own powers), what is shared, the
   review date and the agreement attached. Linked to the ROPA activities whose recipients
   it covers.
9. **The regulator's name.** *Recommendation:* "the Information Commission" wherever the
   law is described; the regulator's published guidance keeps whatever title it is
   published under. *To confirm:* what name the Commission now uses for itself in
   practice.
10. **Part 3 templates.** *Recommendation:* a separate "DPIA (law enforcement, Part 3)"
    template rather than branches in the UK GDPR DPIA: s.64 asks different things
    (categories of data subject, sensitive processing under s.35, logging, s.45 rights
    restrictions), and the two are scored and approved differently in practice.
11. **Order.** *Recommendation:* Phases 0–3 first. They serve every public body — health,
    social care, councils, schools — and are smaller. Then data sharing agreements, then
    Part 3, which only competent authorities need and is the largest.

## Data model

### `entity` additions
- `public_authority` (boolean): a public authority within s.7.
- `competent_authority` (boolean): a competent authority within s.30 for some of its
  functions; makes Part 3 available on its activities.
- `default_regime` (`uk_gdpr` | `dpa_part3`).

### `organisation` / `entity`: the DPO
- `dpo_name`, `dpo_contact`, `dpo_user_id` (optional), `dpo_reports_to`,
  `dpo_published` — on the organisation, overridable per entity.

### `processing_activity` additions
- `regime` (`uk_gdpr` | `dpa_part3`).
- `special_category` (boolean | null), `art9_condition` (the Art 9(2) point),
  `schedule1_condition` (paragraph key).
- `criminal_offence` (boolean | null), `art10_condition` (Schedule 1 paragraph key, or
  official authority).
- Part 3 only: `sensitive_processing` (boolean | null), `schedule8_condition`,
  `subject_categories_part3` (suspects, convicted, victims, witnesses, others),
  `fact_opinion_marked` (boolean), `logging` (jsonb: per system, the operations logged,
  reviewer, last checked).

### New tables
- `policy_document`: the appropriate policy document register (decision 4), with
  `conditions` (Schedule 1 and Schedule 8 keys) and the document attached through the
  existing `document` table.
- `sharing_agreement`: data sharing agreements between controllers (decision 8).

### Requests, breaches
- The `exemption` decision gains a structured `exemption` key (Schedule 2 and 3
  paragraphs, and s.45(4) grounds for Part 3), with the immigration exemption's and
  Schedule 3's extra fields.
- Breaches take the regime of the activities affected; Part 3 runs ss.67–68.

### `lib/dpa2018/` (pure)
- `SCHEDULE_1`, `SCHEDULE_8`, `SCHEDULE_2_EXEMPTIONS`, `SCHEDULE_3`, `PART_3_RESTRICTIONS`:
  each with its paragraph, label and what it requires.
- `conditionGaps(activity, policyDocuments)`, `publicAuthorityGaps(activity, entity)`,
  `part3Gaps(activity)`, `seriousHarmOpinionValid(opinion, now)`.

  stays with the role alone.
- DPIA, edited in place: whether the DPO's advice was sought (or none is designated), and

### Phase 2: Schedule 1 and the appropriate policy document (built)
- The structured Article 9 / Article 10 / Schedule 1 choice on the ROPA and in the DPIA
  (decision 5).
- The policy document register and its gaps (decision 4); para 41's record contents.
- Data categories for social care, education and safeguarding, and a catalogue of
  categories of data subject (patients, service users, pupils, staff; and for Part 3,
  suspects, the convicted, victims, witnesses).

Built as:
- `lib/dpa2018/schedule-1.ts`: the Article 9(2) points, every Schedule 1 condition
  (paragraphs 1–4, 6–28, 29–35) with whether it needs a policy document — paragraph 1,
  all of Part 2 and paragraph 35, with the exceptions in paragraphs 10(2), 13(3) and
  27(3) — and which points need which condition (s.10). Checked against Schedule 1 on
  legislation.gov.uk, up to date to 25 October 2026; paragraph 10 carries the DUAA's
  "investigating".
- On each activity (migration 0079): `special_category`, `art9_condition`,
  `schedule1_condition`, `criminal_offence`, `art10_condition`, and paragraph 41(c)'s
  `retention_follows_policy` with `retention_policy_note`. A Part 1 condition is filled
  in from the Article 9(2) point; a Part 2 one is asked for (g). Criminal offence data
  takes official authority or any Part 1–3 condition, a Part 2 one through paragraph 36.
  What the answers make irrelevant is cleared. Changes are kept in the audit chain.
- Gaps: no Article 9(2) condition, no Schedule 1 condition, no Article 10 condition, and a
  condition needing a policy document that no current one covers — all red. For
  paragraphs 10, 13 and 27 the uncovered gap is amber, because of their exceptions; so is

  withdrawal (para 40(2)). Govern does not track when an activity stopped relying on a

- DPIA, edited in place: the free-text `special_condition` becomes `art9_condition`, then
  `schedule1_condition` for (g); `criminal_offence` and `art10_condition` asked
  separately; and which policy document covers it, where one may be needed.
- The ROPA's categories of data subject and of personal data are ticked from catalogues
  (with others in the record's own words): social care, education and safeguarding data,
  and patients, service users, pupils, parents and carers, staff, and the Part 3
  categories.

### Phase 3: exemptions in requests (built)
- Structured Schedule 2 exemptions; the immigration exemption's case-by-case record and
  notice; Schedule 3 with the serious harm test and the health professional's opinion
  (decision 7).

Built as:
- `lib/dpa2018/exemptions.ts`: the Schedule 2 and 3 paragraphs a request can meet, each
  with the rights it can restrict and its test. Checked against Schedules 2 and 3 on
  legislation.gov.uk, up to date to 29 October 2026 (Sch 2 paras 2 and 3 carry the DUAA's
  "investigation"). Paragraph 5 (disclosure required by law) and paragraph 17 (an
  assumption, not an exemption) are left out.
- On `data_subject_request_decision` (migration 0080): `exemption`, the paragraph, and
  `detail`, what it asks to be recorded; the provision is written from the paragraph. A
  paragraph that does not reach the right decided is refused.
- Immigration (paras 4–4B): the Secretary of State only; one decision per right; how
  vulnerability, rights and duties were weighed; the three para 4A(4) tests confirmed;
  whether the person was told, and why not.
- Health serious harm (Sch 3 para 5): a new decision kind, `health_opinion`, records the
  appropriate health professional's opinion (role, why they are the appropriate one under
  para 2(1), the date, and whether the test is met). A controller that is not a health
  professional can withhold only on an opinion recorded on the same request that the
  test is met and given in the six months ending that day. Decision 7 had an older
  opinion allowed with a reason; the Act does not allow that, so an older one is refused.
  An opinion inside the six months still needs the reason re-consulting would not be
  reasonable (para 5(3)(b)).
- Para 6, the same opinion that the test is *not* met before such a controller discloses
  health data, is shown on access requests as a reminder. Govern cannot know whether an
  answer includes health data, so it does not enforce it.
- Paras 4, 10 and 21: the requester's capacity (parental responsibility, or a court
  appointee); for 4 and 10 which expectation limb; for 21 that it is not Scotland.

### Phase 4: data sharing agreements (built)
- The register (decision 8), linked to the ROPA and the third-party register, with gaps:
  a recipient on a ROPA activity that is another controller with no agreement; an
  agreement past its review date.

Built as:
- The third-party register had no way to say a party is another controller: every third
  party was treated as a processor. Each now has a `role` (processor, another controller,
  or both; migration 0081, defaulting to processor so nothing existing changes). Article
  28 is not applied to a controller, whose standing reads "Another controller: Article 28
  does not apply".
- `sharing_agreement` (our entity, reference `DSA-year-n`, purpose, direction, gateway kind
  and gateway, what is shared, our Article 6 basis with Schedule 1 / Article 10 condition,
  owner, signed, review due, last reviewed, ended), `sharing_agreement_party` (each other
  controller from the third-party register, with its own basis and condition) and
  `sharing_agreement_activity` (the ROPA activities covered, of the same entity). The
  signed agreement is attached through `document`. Rules in `lib/dpa2018/sharing.ts`.
- A party must be recorded as a controller (or both) on the third-party register.
- Agreement gaps: no other party and review overdue first, then a party or us with no
  basis, no purpose, no gateway (unless not a public body), nothing said about what is
  shared, not signed, no review date, nothing attached, no linked activity, no owner. None
  is called a breach: an agreement is expected by the data sharing code, not required by
  the UK GDPR.
- ROPA: an activity whose recipients include a third party recorded as a controller, with
  no current agreement covering that activity and party, shows the amber gap
  `controller_recipient_no_agreement`; the register lists all of them. Recipients are
  matched by canonical name, as the request register matches them.
- Ended rather than deleted, with a reason; an ended agreement covers nothing.

### Phase 5: Part 3, law enforcement processing (built, in two parts)
- **5a, the record:** the competent authority flag and regime (decision 1); s.61 records
  with s.35 sensitive processing, the Schedule 8 condition and the policy document;
  s.38 categories and fact / opinion; logging statements (decision 6); the Part 3 DPIA
  template (decision 10).

5a built as:
- `entity.competent_authority` and `entity.default_regime` (migration 0082), set on the
  Organisation page and audited as `entity.competent_authority_changed`. Turning the flag
  off resets the default to the UK GDPR; records already under Part 3 keep it and are
  flagged red. A competent authority needs a DPO (s.69), flagged as for a public
  authority.
- `processing_activity.regime`, defaulting from the entity, with Part 3 only on a
  competent authority. A Part 3 record asks s.35(2) (task or consent); sensitive
  processing (s.35(8)) with its route (consent, or strictly necessary and a Schedule 8
  condition); fact and opinion (s.38(2)); and a logging statement per system (s.62: the
  six operations, whether consultation and disclosure logs identify who and to whom, the
  reviewer, when last checked). The UK GDPR's questions are hidden on a Part 3 record and
  its answers kept, and the other way round; the retention answer (para 41 / s.42(4)) is
  shared. Rules in `lib/dpa2018/part3.ts`, checked against ss.31, 35, 38, 42, 61, 62 and
  64 and Schedule 8 (up to date to 29–30 October 2026).
- Gaps: not a competent authority, no s.35(2) basis, sensitive processing with no route or
  no Schedule 8 condition, or no current policy document covering it — red. Retention
  against the policy document unanswered, fact and opinion unanswered, s.38(3) categories
  not distinguished, a named system with no logging statement, a statement missing an
  operation or reviewer — amber.
- The policy document register covers Part 3 grounds (consent, and each Schedule 8
  condition) as well as Schedule 1 conditions.
- "Data protection impact assessment (law enforcement, Part 3)" (s.64), offered through
  "Waivern has updated templates" once an entity is a competent authority.
- Not in 5a: Part 3's own further processing (s.36) and automated decisions (ss.50A–50D),
  both built later (below).

- **5b, what happens to it**, split in two: requests and breaches, then automated
  decisions, transfers and s.78A (both built). Rights under ss.44–48 with s.45(4)
  restrictions, recorded reasons and the s.54 clock; breaches under ss.67–68; automated decisions under
  ss.50A–50D on activities and AI use cases; transfers under ss.73–76 in a Part 3
  variant of the transfer assessment; s.78A noted where national security is relied on.

5b requests and breaches built as:
- Checked against Part 3 Chapter 3 (ss.43–54) and ss.67–68 (up to date to 27–31 October
  2026). The DUAA took national security out of the restriction grounds in ss.44(4),
  45(4), 48(3) and 68(7) on 5 September 2025 (it is now s.78A), added s.45A (legal
  professional privilege) the same day, and from 5 February 2026 made s.54's clock run as
  the UK GDPR's does: a month, extendable by two, paused for a reasonably needed
  clarification.
- `data_subject_request.regime` and `breach.regime` (migration 0083), defaulting from the
  entity and Part 3 only on a competent authority. A request starts under Part 3 only if
  every right asked for is one Part 3 gives (access, rectification, erasure or
  restriction, automated decision safeguards), so the public web form never fails; one
  recorded or moved to Part 3 with an objection or portability is refused.
- A new decision kind, `part3_restriction`, records what is restricted (s.44(4), 45(4),
  48(3), or s.45A), the grounds, the necessity and proportionality confirmed, and whether
  the person was told, or why telling them would undermine it; the rationale is the
  record the Commission can ask for. Schedule 2 and 3 exemptions, the health opinion,
  objection and portability are refused on a Part 3 request, and a Part 3 restriction on
  a UK GDPR one. The decision form cites Part 3's sections, and the response must say
  what ss.45(5) and 48(2) require. Rules in `lib/dpa2018/part3-rights.ts`.
- Breaches under Part 3 have the same thresholds and clock; the obligations and decision
  form cite ss.67–68, and the people affected need not be told where s.68(3) applies or
  it is restricted under s.68(7).


5b automated decisions, transfers and s.78A built as:
- Checked against ss.50A–50D, Chapter 5 (ss.72–78), s.78A and s.79 (up to date to 30–31
  October 2026). Section 50C(4) lists national security as a reason to set the safeguards
  aside — unlike the s.45(4) restriction grounds, from which it was removed.
- The automated-decision record on processing activities and AI use cases is read under
  the record's regime. Under Part 3 it cites ss.50A–50C; the sensitive processing
  conditions are explicit consent or required or authorised by law (s.50B), a contract
  being refused; there is no recognised legitimate interests check; and
  `adm_set_aside` (migration 0084) records the s.50C(4) reasons some decisions may be
  taken without the safeguards and how they are reconsidered (s.50C(3)), refused without
  a reconsideration or under the UK GDPR. An AI use case is Part 3 when every activity it
  is linked to is; a copy across a link is checked against the target's regime. Part 3
  activities now show the record and its gaps.
- "Transfer assessment (law enforcement, Part 3)", offered once an entity is a competent
  authority: the three s.73 conditions and member State authorisation (s.73(1)(b), (5),
  (6)); regulations (s.74AA), an instrument or the controller's own view (s.75) with the
  data protection test, or special circumstances (s.76) with the rights override and
  minimisation; the four s.77 conditions for other recipients; documentation and what the
  Commission is told (ss.75(2)–(3), 76(3), 77(7)); onward transfers (s.78); s.78A.
- s.78A as a Part 3 request restriction, needing confirmation that exemption is required
  and recording any s.79 certificate; and on Part 3 breach decisions for ss.67–68.
- The Part 3 DPIA's rights-restriction help no longer lists national security among the
  s.45(4) grounds.

Part 3 further processing, built after 5b:
- Checked against ss.36 and 41 (up to date to 30 October 2026; amended by DUAA ss.71(8)
  and 86(7) from 5 February 2026).
- On a Part 3 record: whether data collected for another law enforcement purpose is
  processed, the original activity, the authorising law (s.36(3)(a)) and necessity and
  proportionality (s.36(3)(b)); research, archiving or statistics with the two s.41(2)
  limits. No authorising law, or recorded as not necessary and proportionate — red;
  proportionality unconfirmed, or a s.41(2) limit unconfirmed — amber. The UK GDPR's
  further processing answers are left as they were.
- On a UK GDPR record whose original activity is under Part 3: the law authorising it
  (s.36(4)), red until given. `further_authorised_by` and
  `further_necessary_proportionate` (migration 0085). Rules in
  `lib/dpa2018/part3-further.ts`.

## Out of scope

- Part 4 (the intelligence services), and ss.82A–82E beyond noting that a designation
  notice exists.
- Freedom of information and the Environmental Information Regulations.
- NHS-specific assurance (the Data Security and Protection Toolkit, Caldicott
  principles, section 251 support): not the Data Protection Act, and worth a plan of
  their own if NHS bodies are a target.
- Holding or analysing system logs (decision 6), or any individual's data: Govern
  records categories and decisions, as everywhere else.
- Deciding a condition, exemption or restriction automatically. As elsewhere in Govern,
  the platform shows what the law asks and a named person records the decision and why.
- The Integration API and the Portal's side, which follow once the records exist.
