01Scope boundary

The clarification round did most of the scoping work. Read together, the BBC’s answers move this decisively away from discovery and towards workflow — which is what makes it buildable, and what the design leans on.

Inside the product

  • One integrated engine covering privacy and AI governance — shared data model, unified interface, consolidated reporting C8 C40 C43
  • AI governance across predictive/classic ML, generative and agentic systems C19 C41
  • Ongoing governance, not point-in-time: recurring reviews, reassessments, remediation tracking C30
  • Jurisdiction-specific TRA and TIA templates, kept current, over a maintained country risk library C15 C23 C36
  • Records classified and reported by legal entity, with entity-scoped access and retention C22
  • Migration of records from an incumbent platform C7

Deliberately outside it

  • Data discovery and content scanning — the structured/unstructured distinction is “not applicable” C3 C6 C13
  • Identity correlation. Recording categories of personal data is sufficient C16
  • Discovery of unregistered or shadow AI usage C17
  • End-to-end supplier assessment. The need is to record completed assessments and evidence C39
  • Automated statistical bias testing. Structured assessment and evidence gathering is the requirement C18

The requirement that shapes the architecture

Buried in the user experience table is “completion without account creation”. The people who actually answer DPIA screening questions are occasional contributors across a large organisation, and requiring each of them to hold an account is the single biggest source of friction in incumbent platforms — and the single biggest driver of seat cost. Supporting it properly changes authentication, the task model and the licensing story at once, so it belongs in the first slice rather than a later one.

Where the spec is silent. Five clarifications went unanswered before the 24 Aug deadline closed: consent management scope and the services feeding it C24 C28, DPA repository volume and whether it must be searchable C25, any timeframe for the future-capability items C26, and the whole sizing and pricing assumption set C35. Consent management is a stated desirable, so the design carries a place for it in the model and defers the build.

02Runtime

Vercel has no long-running worker process. That single fact decides the shape of everything time-based in this product — and almost everything in governance is time-based.

Reassessment cycles, reminder sequences, SLA breach detection and outbound record sync all outlive a request. On the Waivern Compliance Portal these live in an RQ worker on Railway; here they run as durable step functions triggered by cron and by events emitted from route handlers. The practical rule: a request handler never does work it cannot finish, and never does work that must survive a failure. It writes state and emits an event.

Choices and why

  • Next.js App Router + TypeScript. Server components keep record pages fast without shipping the whole data model to the browser, and route handlers give a single deployable surface for both the UI and the public API.
  • Neon Postgres, eu-west-2, functions pinned to lhr1. UK data residency is a hard expectation for a BBC-shaped buyer and a default US region would be a straightforward disqualification.
  • Drizzle over Prisma. Lower cold-start cost on serverless, and migrations that read as SQL — which matters when a compliance buyer asks to see the schema.
  • Inngest for durable work. Steps are individually retried and replayable, so a reminder run that fails halfway does not double-send. Vercel Cron alone gives no durability.
  • Auth.js with OIDC, plus signed single-use task links. Entra ID and Google covered by the same provider path; MFA delegated to the identity provider rather than reimplemented.

03Assessment engine

The BBC says fragmented products with duplicate data entry are “less desirable” C40. The honest way to satisfy that is not to bolt modules together but to make DPIA, TRA, TIA, AI risk and screening the same object with different published templates.

A template is a versioned document with three parts: a schema (sections and typed questions), a logic graph (show, hide and require rules keyed on other answers), and a scoring configuration (likelihood × impact by default, since that is what the BBC uses today, but pluggable because they invited proposals C20). Publishing freezes a version. Every assessment records which version it ran against, so an assessment completed in March remains readable and defensible after the template changes in July — which is what “version history” has to mean in a regulated setting.

What conditional logic has to survive

Question flows are evaluated server-side as well as in the browser. A rule that only runs client-side is a presentation trick; a rule that runs server-side is a validity guarantee, and it is the difference between an assessment record that stands up in an audit and one that does not. Rules are stored as data on the template version, so a hidden question’s answer is recorded as suppressed rather than silently dropped — the auditor needs to see that a section was not applicable, not that it was never there.

04Data model

Nine groups of tables. The ones worth arguing about are the link table, the audit chain and the retention profile — the rest follow from the engine above.

Tenancy and identity

organisation is the Waivern client. entity is a legal entity beneath it — for the BBC, that is BBC Public Service and BBC Studios, explicitly not services like iPlayer or the World Service C22. Every record carries both. Role assignments are scoped to organisation or entity, which is how “entity-specific access controls” becomes enforcement rather than a filter. retention_profile hangs off the entity and varies by record type, matching the BBC’s answer that retention follows their corporate schedule C2.

Records

processing_activity holds the Article 30 record. ai_use_case holds the AI inventory, typed as predictive, generative or agentic. supplier, dpa and supplier_assessment_record cover third-party risk at the recording depth the BBC actually asked for. country_risk is the maintained transfer library — adequacy status, rating, contributing factors, source and a review date, because the requirement is explicitly that it be kept current C36.

The link table

record_link is a typed edge between any two records. It is doing more work than it looks: it satisfies “links between processing activities and assessments” directly, and it is also the entire substrate for AI workflow visualisation. The BBC described that requirement as a view of use cases, assessments, approvals, controls, mitigations, stakeholders and decisions with their status and dependencies C37 — which is a graph. Store the edges properly and the visualisation is a rendering problem, not a data problem.

The audit chain

audit_event is append-only with no update or delete path, and each row carries the hash of its predecessor. Tamper-evidence is cheap to add now and impossible to retrofit credibly, and it turns “exportable audit logs” into an export a third party can independently verify. This mirrors the append-only discipline already in the Waivern Portal.

Assessment and risk

Answers are stored per question rather than as one document blob, so two contributors can hold different parts of the same assessment and the audit trail attributes each answer to a person and a moment. assessment_revision snapshots on every submit. risk carries inherent and residual scores; mitigation hangs off it with an owner, due date and evidence link; risk_acceptance records who accepted, on what rationale, and when it expires — an acceptance without an expiry is how risk registers rot.

05Workflow and time

The BBC wants both point-in-time assessments and ongoing oversight C30. Those are different mechanisms and the design keeps them separate.

Tasks and the no-account path

A task can be assigned to a user or to a bare email address. The second case issues a signed, single-use, expiring link that grants access to exactly one assessment section and nothing else. The contributor answers their part, it lands attributed to their email, and no account is ever created. This is the mechanism behind “completion without account creation”, and it is also why Waivern can price on governed records rather than seats.

Approvals and thresholds

A workflow definition maps a template kind to an ordered set of stages, each with a routing rule. Rules read from the assessment: residual score above a threshold, special-category data present, transfer to a non-adequate country, AI system classified high-risk. Approvers approve, reject, or return for changes with a mandatory rationale. The rationale is not optional anywhere in this system.

Scheduling

A schedule attaches a recurrence to a record and materialises tasks ahead of the due date — recurring reviews, reassessments, attestations, policy reviews, control checks. The BBC was specific that this is governance-activity scheduling and explicitly not a calendaring product C38, so there is no calendar surface, only due dates, reminders and completion state. SLA policies are separate: a target duration per task type, a breach sweep on the durable path, and an escalation route.

06The AI layer

Advisory only. The model proposes; a named human decides and attests. This is a deliberate product position, not a limitation.

Model output never writes to a decision field. Suggestions land in an ai_suggestion record carrying the model, the prompt version and the input snapshot, and a human either accepts it into the record or does not — with the acceptance itself audited. Four uses earn their place: proposing a risk rating from the answers given, drafting candidate mitigations, flagging internal contradictions or gaps in a submitted assessment, and summarising a portfolio for an executive view.

Every suggestion biases towards escalation: where the answers are ambiguous, the proposal is the higher risk rating and the recommendation is to escalate. Under UK GDPR Article 35 the controller carries the assessment; a platform that lets a model quietly set a residual score has moved accountability somewhere it cannot legally sit. Vendors in this market are increasingly willing to blur that line, and refusing to is worth stating plainly in the RFI response.

Practically, this also means the request-path model calls must fail soft. The Waivern Portal already learned that the hard way: JSON is coerced through the prompt rather than assistant prefill, parsing recovers fenced and prose-wrapped output, and an unparseable response returns an empty suggestion set rather than failing the user’s save. The same discipline applies here — a governance record must always be savable, whether or not the advisory layer answered.

07Portal seam

Two products, two clouds, one versioned contract. The Compliance Portal discovers and generates; Govern routes, decides and records. Neither reaches into the other’s database.

Concretely, the portal’s vendor upsert and DPA extractor already produce records shaped almost exactly like Govern’s supplier and dpa tables, and its scan output is natural evidence to attach to a DPIA. In the other direction, an approved assessment or an accepted risk is exactly the kind of confirmed fact the portal’s context streams and document generator want, and it arrives with a human attestation attached — which is what makes it safe to generate from.

The contract is versioned from the first release (/api/v1/) and the two products deploy independently. That is the whole point: they can be sold together or apart, and a client running only Govern is not carrying dead weight.

09Build sequence

Five phases to a working spine by 1 September, in dependency order — each one is only useful once the one above it exists.

  1. Phase 1 FoundationRepository, Next.js and Drizzle against Neon in eu-west-2, OIDC sign-in, organisation and entity tenancy, role scoping, and the hash-chained audit primitive every later phase writes through. Thu
  2. Phase 2 Template engineSchema, logic graph and scoring configuration; the authoring surface; publish-and-freeze versioning; seeded DPIA, UK TRA, EU TIA, AI risk and screening templates with linked legal references. Thu–Fri
  3. Phase 3 Assessment runtimeFilling an assessment with server-side conditional logic, per-question attribution, revision snapshots, and the signed single-use link that lets a contributor complete a section without an account. Fri–Sat
  4. Phase 4 Risk and decisionRegister with inherent and residual scoring, mitigations with owners and evidence, and risk acceptance carrying an attesting human, a rationale and an expiry. Sat–Sun
  5. Phase 5 Workflow and timeTasks and assignment, threshold-routed approval stages, SLA policies and breach escalation, recurrence schedules, and the Inngest steps that drive reminders and reassessment. Sun–Mon
  6. Stretch Demo surfaceA BBC-shaped seed tenant — two legal entities, realistic assessments in flight — plus a first dashboard and the Portal ingest endpoints. Cut first if the spine runs late. Mon

Reporting screens, the RoPA editing surface, third-party risk and the AI workflow graph land after 1 September. That is a deliberate cut, not an oversight: the spine is what every one of them is a view over, and building views before the thing they view is how this kind of project ends up rewritten.

10Open risks

The RFI cannot be won on this build alone

The BBC stated plainly that it is not looking to build a bespoke platform and wants mature, market-proven products with minimal custom development C33. A five-day-old codebase does not answer that, and pretending otherwise in a response would be transparent. What this build does earn is a credible demo — and since any future procurement includes a demo but explicitly no proof of value, pilot or trial C4, the demo is the only technical evidence that will ever be seen.

The country risk library is a content commitment

“Out of the box templates per jurisdiction which are current” C15 and country information that “must be maintained and kept up to date” C36 are ongoing editorial obligations, not features that ship once. Whoever answers this requirement is committing to a maintenance function. That should be priced and staffed explicitly rather than absorbed.

Migration is in scope and unsized

The platform replaces an incumbent and existing records require migration C7, but the incumbent is not named and volumes were never given C35. The template-version model helps here — historical records can be imported against a frozen “as-migrated” template rather than forced into a current one — but the effort remains genuinely unknown and any indicative cost must say so.

Consent management is a stated desirable with no scope

Two clarifications asking which systems feed it went unanswered C24 C28. The model reserves room for it; the build does not attempt it. Guessing here would be expensive and probably wrong.
