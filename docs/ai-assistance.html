Waivern Govern · Annexe to §07, The AI layer

Conversational assistance in a governance workflow

Where a chat-style assistant belongs in this platform, where it must not go, and what has to be true of the architecture before either question is worth answering.

Prepared for the BBC Privacy and AI Governance Platform RFI Extends §07 of the design document

The position, and what this annexe adds

The model proposes. A named human decides and attests. Model output never writes to a decision field.

That commitment is made in §07 and is not revisited here. What §07 describes, though, is inline suggestion: proposals derived from answers already captured in the platform, offered against a specific field, recorded in an ai_suggestion row and accepted or discarded by a person.

A conversational interface is a materially different thing, and the difference is not the interaction style. It is the direction of data flow. Inline suggestion reads from records the organisation has already chosen to hold. A chat box is an open ingress: it invites a user to type whatever they think is relevant, and some of them will type a claimant's name, a colleague's health condition, or the contents of an incident report.

A governance platform that opens an unminimised channel to a third-party model has created a processing activity it did not record, in a place its own registers cannot see. That is the risk this annexe exists to address, and it shapes every recommendation below.

The rule that decides placement

The platform already refuses to decide anything. Routing rules escalate rather than answer when a question is unanswerable; an empty country library flags every transfer rather than clearing it; a scanner finding becomes a risk only when a person converts it. An assistant has to inherit that posture or it becomes a route around it.

The test

If a routing rule or the scheduled sweep would escalate a question rather than answer it, the assistant may not answer it either. It may explain what the question means, and it may draft what an answer could look like. It may not supply the judgement.

Stated as a boundary: the assistant may draft, explain and find. It may never attest, decide, or close a gap.

How a contribution travels

Every conversational output follows the same path, whichever surface it came from. The step in colour is the only one that changes the record, and it is always a person.

Ask User question, plus minimised record context
Propose Model returns a draft, an explanation, or nothing
Hold Stored as a suggestion with model, prompt version and inputs
Accept A named person edits and commits it. This is the audited act

The suggestion record is retained whether or not it was accepted. A proposal that a reviewer rejected is evidence of diligence, and deleting it would leave the audit trail showing only the proposals that happened to be agreed with.

Where it earns its place

Ranked by value against risk. The first two share all of their infrastructure, which is why they are proposed as one increment.

  1. 01 Beside a question, while answering an assessment This is where the non-specialist personas actually stall. An engineering lead facing “does this involve special category data?” is blocked on vocabulary, not effort. Most acute on no-account contributor links, where the person has one screen and no surrounding context to learn from. Outputs — plain-language explanation · draft answer text · what evidence would satisfy this
  2. 02 Natural-language questions about the platform The in-product help is already a structured corpus of twenty-two topics covering every screen and the reasoning behind it. It gives retrieval a defined ground truth, so answers can be grounded in shipped documentation rather than generated from the model's general knowledge of privacy law. Outputs — an answer citing the help topic it came from · no writes
  3. 03 Drafting a register entry from a description Turning “we send viewing history to a US analytics vendor” into proposed purposes, data categories, recipients and a transfer with its destination. Converts a blank Article 30 form into an editing task, which is a far lower barrier. Each field is proposed separately and accepted separately. Outputs — per-field suggestions · never a wholesale record write
  4. 04 “What is this, and why has it come to me?” Contextual explanation on a task, risk or finding. The product manager's real question, and currently answerable only by asking a privacy colleague. Combines the record with the governance reason it exists. Outputs — explanation only
  5. 05 Explaining a scanner finding What a pre-consent cookie from an unfamiliar vendor implies, and what would need to be true for it to matter. Conversion into a risk remains a human act, as it is today. Outputs — explanation · candidate risk wording. Never a materiality verdict
  6. 06 Wording for risks and mitigations Turning “it might leak” into a properly framed risk statement with candidate treatments. Wording only; the likelihood and impact ratings stay with the person. Outputs — draft text for description and mitigation fields
  7. 07 Reviewing a template against a checklist “Does this DPIA cover Article 35(7)(a) to (d)?” Newly practical, because template definitions are now readable in the interface with their conditional logic rendered in English. Outputs — gap list against a named checklist

Where it must not go

Each of these is a point where the platform already declines to decide. An assistant answering them would not be adding a feature; it would be removing a control.

  • Setting a likelihood, impact or residual rating The judgement the register exists to record. Under Article 35 the controller carries the assessment; a platform that lets a model set a residual score has moved accountability somewhere it cannot legally sit.
  • Deciding an approval gate, or accepting a risk Both sit with the approver role alone, deliberately separated from the ability to edit the record. An assistant reaching them would collapse that separation.
  • Determining a screening outcome Whether a DPIA is required is the decision the screening template exists to put to a person.
  • Setting adequacy status, or confirming a processor Both are attestations that a person checked something against a source. A model asserting them produces a register that looks verified and is not.

What has to change first

One gap has to close before any of this is defensible, and it is small. The audit chain records an actor kind of user, contributor_link, system or integration. There is no value for an assistant.

Without one, an answer drafted by a model and accepted by a person is indistinguishable in the audit trail from one that person wrote themselves. For a platform whose central claim is an unbroken record of who decided what, that is the first question a regulator would ask and the one currently unanswerable. Provenance comes before surfaces.

  • An assistant actor kind, so a proposal is attributable to a model and a version, distinctly from the person who accepted it.
  • Acceptance recorded as its own act, carrying both the suggestion it came from and the human who took responsibility for it.
  • Conversation retention scoped and disclosed, since a multi-turn exchange accumulates context that no single record shows.

A model of the client's choosing

The platform already stores per-organisation integration secrets encrypted at rest with AES-256-GCM, and already scopes each connection to the endpoints its kind is entitled to use. A model provider is another connection kind on that existing mechanism rather than new infrastructure.

  • Provider adapters behind one interface — Azure OpenAI, Amazon Bedrock, Anthropic, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint the organisation hosts itself. The BBC would be expected to require its own tenancy, and this shape gives it without a code change.
  • Off by default, and off per surface. An organisation that enables drafting assistance on assessments is not thereby enabling it on the risk register.
  • Minimisation as a control, not an optimisation. What is sent is the question and the organisation's own prose. Never the register wholesale, never identities. What was sent is as auditable as what came back.
  • Fail soft, always. JSON is coerced through the prompt rather than assistant prefill, parsing recovers fenced and prose-wrapped output, and an unparseable response yields an empty suggestion rather than blocking a save. A governance record must remain savable whether or not the advisory layer answered.
Self-application

Where an organisation enables this, the platform records its own use of AI in its own register — system, provenance, lifecycle stage, oversight model, and the transfer if the endpoint sits outside the UK. A governance platform that does not govern the AI inside itself invites the obvious question, and answering it in advance is a stronger position than being asked.

Residual risks, stated plainly

These do not have clean engineering answers. They are stated because a buyer should hear them from the supplier rather than discover them.

Anchoring on a proposed rating

§07 admits proposing a risk rating among the four permitted uses, with bias towards escalation. Escalation bias controls the direction of error but not the anchoring: a reviewer shown “high” is measurably more likely to accept high. Mitigations are presentational — proposing a range rather than a point, requiring the rationale field before acceptance — and none removes the effect. It should be measured in pilot rather than assumed away.

Free-text ingress

Users will type things into a chat box that they would not enter into a form. Detection and redaction of obvious identifiers before a request leaves the platform is the practical control, together with a plainly worded standing notice at the point of entry. Neither is complete, and a control that is not complete should be described as partial.

Context accumulating across turns

A conversation gathers material from several records into one exchange that no single record reflects. Retention, scope and the reader's right of access to it need to be settled before launch, not after.

Proposed increments

Estimates assume the existing codebase, and are given for the build only.

IncrementContentsEffort
Provenance Assistant actor kind, suggestion and acceptance records, retention policy. Prerequisite for everything else. 1 day
Provider layer Adapter interface, per-organisation configuration on the existing encrypted-secret mechanism, per-surface switches, minimisation and fail-soft parsing. 1 day
First two surfaces Assistance while answering an assessment, and natural-language help grounded in the shipped corpus. 1–2 days
Register drafting Article 30 and AI register entries proposed field by field from a description. 1 day

Sequenced so that the first two increments are useful on their own: they are what makes any later surface auditable, and they are the part a buyer should hold the supplier to before any assistant is switched on.
